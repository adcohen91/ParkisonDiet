# Parkinson's Diet — Knowledge Base

This document is the primary knowledge source for generating personalised meal plans for people with Parkinson's Disease and Parkinson's Dysautonomia.

---

## Dietary Philosophy

The diet type for each plan is **strictly determined by the user profile** provided at generation time. Do not impose a vegan, plant-based, or any other diet unless the user has explicitly selected it.

All plans are:
- **Whole-food, minimally processed** — prioritise natural ingredients
- **Low sodium** — to support blood pressure management in Dysautonomia
- **Soft-friendly** — textures that are manageable for people with motor symptoms
- **Easy to prepare** — minimal complex cooking steps

When the user has not selected vegan or any meat exclusions, include meat, fish, poultry, dairy, and eggs freely as appropriate for a balanced Parkinson's-friendly diet.

---

## Parkinson's & Dysautonomia Nutritional Context

### Blood Pressure & Autonomic Function
Dysautonomia commonly causes orthostatic hypotension (sudden blood pressure drop when standing). Dietary strategies:
- Eat smaller, more frequent meals to prevent postprandial hypotension
- Maintain consistent fluid intake throughout the day
- Avoid high-sodium processed foods
- Include adequate complex carbohydrates to maintain steady glucose

### Dopamine Precursor Foods
These foods naturally support dopamine synthesis:
- Red and green lentils (L-dopa, tyrosine)
- Green peas and split peas
- Pumpkin seeds (tyrosine-rich)
- Bananas (dopamine and serotonin precursors)
- Note: If taking levodopa, time high-protein meals carefully — consult neurologist

### Anti-Inflammatory Priorities
Feature daily: blueberries and mixed berries (anthocyanins), ground flaxseed and chia seeds (ALA omega-3), walnuts, turmeric + black pepper, ginger, extra virgin olive oil, kale and spinach.

### Gut Health & Motility
Constipation is common in Parkinson's Dysautonomia. Support with:
- High dietary fibre: oats, beans, lentils, vegetables, fruits
- Fermented foods: white miso paste
- Hydrating foods: soups, broths, smoothie bowls
- Prebiotic foods: garlic, onions, oats

### Key Micronutrients
- **Vitamin B12**: nutritional yeast, fortified oat milk
- **Iron**: lentils, spinach, pumpkin seeds, chickpeas
- **Omega-3**: flaxseed, chia seeds, walnuts, hemp seeds
- **Calcium**: fortified oat milk, kale, white beans, tahini
- **Magnesium**: pumpkin seeds, dark leafy greens, oats, bananas
- **Folate**: chickpeas, lentils, spinach

## Common Allergen Substitutions

| Allergen | Substitute |
|---|---|
| Nuts (tree nuts, peanuts) | Seeds (sunflower, pumpkin, hemp, chia) |
| Soy (tofu, miso, tamari) | Chickpeas, white beans, coconut aminos |
| Gluten/Wheat | Rice, quinoa, oat flour (certified GF), potato |
| Sesame | Sunflower seed butter, tahini-free dressings |

## Meal Plan Generation Rules

1. **Diet is user-specified**: the Diet field in the user profile overrides everything in this knowledge base. If the user is not vegan, do not produce vegan meals — use meat, fish, poultry, dairy, and eggs as appropriate.
2. **No repetition**: do not repeat the same meal within a 3-day window
3. **Variety**: rotate protein sources and grain bases (rice, quinoa, pasta, oats, noodles)
4. **Balance**: each day should have something raw/fresh and something warm
5. **Seasonal preference**: prefer ingredients that are widely available year-round
6. **Allergen-strict**: if any allergen is flagged, it must not appear in any form (including sauces, seasonings)
7. **Budget-aware**: lean toward economical staples when budget is tight
8. **Dysautonomia adjustment**: if diagnosed, emphasise smaller, more frequent meals; add mid-morning and mid-afternoon snack suggestions
9. **Tags per meal**: always include 2–3 from ["High Fiber", "Anti-Inflammatory", "Quick", "Plant-Based", "Gut Health", "Brain Health", "Easy Prep", "Rich in Antioxidants"]
