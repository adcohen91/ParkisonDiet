Nutrition & Parkinson's Dysautonomia

What is Dysautonomia with Parkinson's?
Dysautonomia affects the autonomic nervous system — regulating blood pressure, digestion, and temperature. Diet plays a significant role in managing symptoms.
🫀 Blood Pressure & Hydration
Dysautonomia can cause orthostatic hypotension. This plan avoids high-sodium processed foods. Hydrate consistently. Eat smaller, more frequent meals to avoid postprandial drops in blood pressure.
🧠 Dopamine Precursors
Broad beans (fava), peas, and lentils contain L-dopa and tyrosine — natural dopamine precursors. However, high-protein foods can compete with levodopa absorption. This plan spaces protein and any medications appropriately.
🌿 Anti-Inflammatory Focus
Berries, flaxseed, walnuts, turmeric, ginger, leafy greens, and extra virgin olive oil are featured throughout. These combat neuroinflammation and oxidative stress linked to Parkinson's progression.
💩 Gut Health & Motility
Constipation is common in Parkinson's Dysautonomia. This plan is rich in fiber (oats, beans, vegetables, fruits), fermented foods (sauerkraut, miso), and hydrating foods to support gut motility.
⚡ Key Nutrients Included
B12: Nutritional yeast, fortified plant milk · Iron: Lentils, spinach, pumpkin seeds · Omega-3: Flaxseed, chia, walnuts · Calcium: Fortified oat milk, kale, white beans · Magnesium: Pumpkin seeds, dark chocolate, spinach · Folate: Chickpeas, lentils, asparagus
⚠️ Important Notes
Always consult your neurologist or dietitian regarding meal timing with medications like levodopa. Vitamin K in leafy greens should be consistent if on blood thinners. Fava beans contain natural levodopa and should be discussed with your doctor if you take levodopa medications.
