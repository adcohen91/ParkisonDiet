1. Overview & Purpose
This document is a complete dietary requirements specification for a 26-day plant-based meal plan covering June 5 through June 30, 2026. It is designed for a 61-year-old individual living with Parkinson's Disease and Dysautonomia, working within a $100 grocery budget.
The plan is 100% vegan — free of all meat and dairy — and is crafted to support neurological health, autonomic nervous system function, gut motility, and anti-inflammatory nutrition requirements specific to Parkinson's Dysautonomia.

1.1 Dietary Constraints
No meat of any kind (beef, poultry, pork, seafood, or by-products)
No dairy of any kind (milk, cheese, butter, yogurt, whey, casein)
No eggs
Low sodium where possible (supports blood pressure management)
Whole food, minimally processed ingredients
Budget ceiling: $100 total for the period June 5 to June 30, 2026

1.2 Health Context
Parkinson's Dysautonomia — Key Dietary Considerations
Dysautonomia affects the autonomic nervous system, impacting blood pressure regulation, digestion, and temperature control. Dietary choices play a significant role in symptom management. Key priorities include: anti-inflammatory nutrition, support for dopamine precursors, gut motility promotion, blood pressure stability through meal timing and hydration, and avoidance of processed high-sodium foods.


Orthostatic hypotension: eat smaller, more frequent meals; stay well hydrated
Dopamine support: include lentils, peas, and fava beans (natural L-dopa precursors)
Gut health: high fiber, fermented foods (miso), and consistent hydration
Neuroinflammation: berries, flaxseed, walnuts, turmeric, ginger, and olive oil throughout
Medication timing: high-protein meals may compete with levodopa absorption — consult your neurologist regarding meal and medication timing

3. Full Recipe Collection
All recipes are plant-based, dairy-free, and meat-free. Ingredients and steps are optimized for ease of preparation and nutritional density. Click on any recipe title in the Table of Contents to navigate directly.

3.1  Blueberry Oat Porridge
Type: Breakfast
Prep/Cook Time: 10 min
Serves: 1


Ingredients
1 cup rolled oats
2 cups oat milk
1/2 cup blueberries
1 tbsp ground flaxseed
1 tbsp almond butter
1 tsp maple syrup
Pinch of cinnamon

Instructions
Bring oat milk to a gentle simmer in a small saucepan.
Add oats and stir, cooking on medium-low for 5 minutes until creamy.
Stir in flaxseed and cinnamon.
Top with blueberries, almond butter, and a drizzle of maple syrup.

3.2  Red Lentil Dal
Type: Dinner
Prep/Cook Time: 30 min
Serves: 4


Ingredients
1.5 cups red lentils
1 can diced tomatoes
1 large onion diced
3 cloves garlic
1 tbsp fresh ginger
1 tsp turmeric
1 tsp cumin
1 tsp coriander
2 cups spinach
2 cups brown rice
Salt to taste
Cilantro to garnish

Instructions
Rinse lentils. Cook onion in a large pot with a splash of water for 5 minutes.
Add garlic, ginger, and spices; cook 1 minute.
Add lentils, tomatoes, and 3 cups water. Simmer 20 minutes until lentils are soft.
Stir in spinach and cook 2 more minutes.
Serve over cooked brown rice and garnish with cilantro.

3.3  Black Bean & Sweet Potato Tacos
Type: Dinner
Prep/Cook Time: 35 min
Serves: 4


Ingredients
2 medium sweet potatoes cubed
1 can black beans drained
8 corn tortillas
1 tsp cumin
1 tsp smoked paprika
1/2 tsp chili powder
Salsa
Lime juice
Avocado slices
Cilantro

Instructions
Toss sweet potato cubes with spices and roast at 400F for 25 minutes until tender.
Warm black beans in a pan with a little cumin and salt.
Warm tortillas in a dry pan.
Assemble tacos with sweet potato, beans, avocado, salsa, cilantro, and lime.

3.4  Chia Pudding with Mango
Type: Breakfast
Prep/Cook Time: 5 min + overnight
Serves: 2


Ingredients
1/3 cup chia seeds
1.5 cups oat milk
1 tbsp maple syrup
1/2 tsp vanilla extract
1 ripe mango diced

Instructions
Mix chia seeds, oat milk, maple syrup, and vanilla in a jar.
Stir well, let sit 5 minutes, stir again.
Cover and refrigerate overnight or at least 4 hours.
Top with fresh mango before serving.

3.5  Chickpea & Tomato Stew
Type: Dinner
Prep/Cook Time: 25 min
Serves: 4


Ingredients
2 cans chickpeas drained
1 can crushed tomatoes
1 bunch kale chopped
1 onion diced
4 cloves garlic
1 tsp smoked paprika
1 tsp cumin
1/2 tsp oregano
Olive oil
Salt & pepper
Whole grain bread

Instructions
Saute onion in olive oil until soft. Add garlic and spices.
Add crushed tomatoes and chickpeas. Simmer 12 minutes.
Stir in kale, cook until wilted about 3 minutes.
Season with salt and pepper. Serve with whole grain bread.

3.6  Miso Soup with Tofu & Soba Noodles
Type: Dinner
Prep/Cook Time: 20 min
Serves: 3


Ingredients
4 cups water
3 tbsp white miso paste
200g silken tofu cubed
100g soba noodles
2 cups baby spinach
2 scallions sliced
1 tsp sesame oil

Instructions
Cook soba noodles per package; drain and set aside.
Bring water to near-boil (do NOT boil). Reduce heat.
Whisk miso into water until dissolved.
Add tofu, spinach, and scallions; heat gently 2 minutes.
Divide noodles into bowls, ladle soup over, drizzle sesame oil.

3.7  Lentil & Vegetable Soup
Type: Dinner
Prep/Cook Time: 40 min
Serves: 6


Ingredients
1.5 cups green lentils
3 carrots sliced
3 stalks celery
1 onion diced
3 cloves garlic
1 can diced tomatoes
1 tsp cumin
1 tsp thyme
1 bay leaf
5 cups vegetable broth
Lemon juice
Salt & pepper

Instructions
Saute onion, carrots, celery in a large pot for 5 minutes.
Add garlic, cumin, thyme, bay leaf; cook 1 minute.
Add lentils, tomatoes, and broth. Bring to boil then simmer 30 minutes.
Remove bay leaf. Squeeze lemon juice over. Season and serve.

3.8  Stir-Fried Tofu with Broccoli & Brown Rice
Type: Dinner
Prep/Cook Time: 25 min
Serves: 3


Ingredients
300g firm tofu pressed & cubed
3 cups broccoli florets
2 cups brown rice cooked
3 tbsp tamari low sodium
1 tbsp rice vinegar
1 tbsp sesame oil
2 cloves garlic
1 tsp fresh ginger
1 tsp cornstarch
Sesame seeds

Instructions
Press tofu dry. Pan-fry in a non-stick pan until golden on all sides.
Mix tamari, vinegar, cornstarch, and 2 tbsp water for sauce.
Stir-fry garlic and ginger 30 seconds, add broccoli, cook 4 minutes.
Add tofu, pour sauce over, toss 2 minutes until glossy.
Serve over brown rice, sprinkle with sesame seeds.

1/2 cup blueberries
1/3 cup maple syrup

Fold in blueberries.
Fill muffin cups 3/4 full. Bake 22 minutes until toothpick clean.

5. Nutritional Requirements & Health Notes
This section outlines the nutritional framework of the meal plan and its alignment with the specific health requirements of Parkinson's Disease with Dysautonomia.

5.1 Blood Pressure & Autonomic Function
Orthostatic Hypotension Management
Dysautonomia commonly causes orthostatic hypotension — a sudden drop in blood pressure when standing. Dietary strategies: eat smaller, more frequent meals to prevent postprandial hypotension; maintain consistent fluid intake throughout the day; avoid high-sodium processed foods; include adequate complex carbohydrates to maintain steady glucose.


5.2 Dopamine Precursor Foods
Several foods in this plan naturally support dopamine synthesis through L-dopa and tyrosine content:
Red and green lentils — featured in dal, soups, shepherd's pie, and stews
Green peas and split peas — dopamine precursor content
Pumpkin seeds — tyrosine-rich
Bananas — dopamine and serotonin precursors
Important Medication Note
If you take levodopa (e.g., Sinemet), high-protein meals can compete with its absorption in the gut and brain. Consult your neurologist about timing protein-heavy dinners relative to your medication schedule. Fava beans (broad beans) contain significant natural levodopa — discuss inclusion with your doctor if you take levodopa medications.


5.3 Anti-Inflammatory Nutrition
Neuroinflammation and oxidative stress are implicated in Parkinson's progression. This plan incorporates the following anti-inflammatory foods daily:
Berries (blueberries, mixed berries) — anthocyanins and polyphenols
Ground flaxseed and chia seeds — ALA omega-3 fatty acids
Walnuts — omega-3s, polyphenols, vitamin E
Turmeric with black pepper — curcumin bioavailability enhanced by piperine
Ginger — gingerols with neuroprotective properties
Extra virgin olive oil — oleocanthal anti-inflammatory compound
Dark leafy greens (kale, spinach) — vitamin K, folate, and antioxidants

5.4 Gut Health & Motility
Constipation and gut dysmotility are common and distressing symptoms in Parkinson's Dysautonomia. This plan addresses gut health through:
High dietary fiber: oats, beans, lentils, vegetables, and fruits throughout
Fermented foods: white miso paste provides beneficial probiotics
Hydrating foods: smoothie bowls, soups, and broths support fluid intake
Prebiotic foods: garlic, onions, and oats feed beneficial gut bacteria
Consistent meal timing: supports circadian gut motility regulation

6. Practical Preparation Tips
6.1 Meal Prep Strategy
Batch cook brown rice and lentils at the start of each week — refrigerate for up to 5 days
Prepare overnight oats or chia pudding the night before for effortless mornings
Make a large pot of soup on Sundays — most soups keep 4-5 days refrigerated and freeze well
Bake banana-blueberry oat muffins (Day 23) in a large batch; freeze extras for grab-and-go breakfasts
Roast a sheet pan of sweet potatoes, cauliflower, and bell peppers at the start of each week
Keep ripe bananas in the freezer for quick smoothie bowls

6.2 Fatigue & Motor Symptom Accommodation
Use a rice cooker with a timer to have grains ready without standing over the stove
Pre-portion spices into small bowls before cooking to reduce in-kitchen complexity
Slow cooker soups and stews (lentil dal, bean soups) allow passive cooking with minimal effort
Frozen vegetables are equally nutritious and eliminate chopping when energy is low
Smoothie bowls require only a blender — keep ingredients pre-portioned in the freezer

6.3 Food Safety & Storage
Cool cooked food within 2 hours; refrigerate in airtight containers
Cooked beans and lentils: 5 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen
Tofu after opening: use within 3-4 days; change water daily
Avocados: store whole; once cut, squeeze lemon juice on cut surface to slow browning
Fresh berries: use within 2-3 days or freeze immediately

6.4 Hydration
Dysautonomia significantly affects hydration needs. Recommendations:
Aim for 8-10 cups of water daily, spread consistently throughout the day
Soups and broths count toward fluid intake
Avoid drinking large amounts of fluid with meals (can worsen postprandial hypotension)
Herbal teas (ginger, peppermint, chamomile) support gut motility and hydration
